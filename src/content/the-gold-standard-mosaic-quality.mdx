# The Gold Standard: What Defines High-Resolution Mosaic Quality for Large Prints

Your memories deserve more than a blurry, pixelated print. When you're transforming thousands of photos into a single masterpiece, "high resolution" isn't just a technical spec—it's the difference between a cluttered mess and a gallery-grade work of art.

Whether you're planning a wedding gift or a corporate installation, understanding what makes a mosaic "high quality" is the first step to a perfect result.

## 1. Perceptual Color Mapping vs. Basic Averaging

Most free mosaic tools use "basic averaging." They look at a small area of your main photo, find its average color, and slap a tile that matches that hex code. The result? A flat, muddy image that loses the "soul" of the original.

**The Photomosaic Way:** We use *perceptual color mapping*. Our engine analyzes the shapes, textures, and gradients in your source image. This ensures that every tile contributes to the clarity of the larger picture, preserving the fine details in eyes, hair, and horizons.

## 2. The Sweet Spot of Tile Density

You might think more tiles always mean more detail. But there's a limit. If your tiles are too small, they turn into "noise" (colored dots) and lose their individual story. If they're too large, the main image looks blocky.

**The Pro Secret:** For a large 24"x36" print, we recommend between 1,000 and 3,000 tiles. This ensures that when you stand back, the main image is crisp, but when you step forward, every individual memory is clearly visible.

## 3. The "Squint Test"

Here’s how pros evaluate a mosaic:
1. **Up Close:** Can you recognize the faces in the small tiles? If they're blurry, the source resolution was too low.
2. **From a Distance:** Does the main image pop?
3. **The Squint:** Close your eyes halfway. Does the mosaic still look like the original photo? If the colors look "off," the software failed the color mapping stage.

## 4. Preparing for the Big Print

To get that "WOW" factor on your wall, your final file needs to be massive.
- **Resolution:** At least 300 DPI (Dots Per Inch) at the final print size.
- **File Size:** Expect a high-quality mosaic export to be 50MB to 200MB.
- **Color Space:** Professional printers prefer CMYK, but a high-quality RGB export works perfectly for most modern canvas and metal prints.
